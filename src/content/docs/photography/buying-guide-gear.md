<div class="space-y-5 min-w-0">

Whether you’re just getting into photography or upgrading from an older camera, trying to make a good decision is very hard - camera gear is expensive, and you don’t want to regret your purchase in a few months’ time. This section will aim to provide a few suggestions for cameras in different price brackets, as well as help you look for and decide what features are important to you. Remember, just because a camera isn’t mentioned here doesn’t mean it isn’t good. Try to learn what matters to you, and if a camera checks all those boxes for a reasonable price, then go for it!

</div>

Before choosing a model

## Answer these four questions

Your priorities depend on your budget and the photos you take. Sports, street, and travel photographers need different things.

<div aria-label="Questions to answer before buying" class="grid gap-px overflow-hidden border border-white/10 bg-white/10 sm:grid-cols-2 xl:grid-cols-4">

<div class="space-y-3 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<span class="font-['Space_Mono',monospace] text-[0.72rem] font-bold tracking-[0.14em] text-emerald-300 uppercase">Budget</span>
<strong class="block text-neutral-100!">How much can you spend?</strong>

Include the lens, batteries, memory cards, and a bag. A cheaper body with a good lens is often the better buy.

</div>

<div class="space-y-3 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<span class="font-['Space_Mono',monospace] text-[0.72rem] font-bold tracking-[0.14em] text-emerald-300 uppercase">Subject</span>
<strong class="block text-neutral-100!">What do you shoot?</strong>

Sports and wildlife need fast autofocus. Street and travel work well with small kits. Portraits and events often need better lenses and low-light performance.

</div>

<div class="space-y-3 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<span class="font-['Space_Mono',monospace] text-[0.72rem] font-bold tracking-[0.14em] text-emerald-300 uppercase">Size</span>
<strong class="block text-neutral-100!">Will you carry it often?</strong>

A small camera you carry is more useful than a better camera left at home.

</div>

<div class="space-y-3 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<span class="font-['Space_Mono',monospace] text-[0.72rem] font-bold tracking-[0.14em] text-emerald-300 uppercase">Mount</span>
<strong class="block text-neutral-100!">Which lenses fit?</strong>

The lens mount controls which lenses you can use. Check lens prices before choosing a body, especially for a full-frame system.

</div>

</div>

### <span id="entry-2807b3bf66c380a8b158fb44e1bb45b2"></span>Megapixels

<dl class="space-y-3">

<div class="space-y-1">

<dt class="font-mono text-xs tracking-wider uppercase text-neutral-400">Importance</dt><dd class="m-0 text-sm leading-7 text-neutral-300">Nice to have</dd>

</div>

<div class="space-y-1">

<dt class="font-mono text-xs tracking-wider uppercase text-neutral-400">Notes</dt><dd class="m-0 text-sm leading-7 text-neutral-300">1MP = 1,000,000 pixels. A very common misconception that many people draw from reading spec sheets and advertisements is that more megapixels = better images. However, this is not the case: the iPhone 17 Pro has a 48MP main sensor, while the Nikon Z8 has a 45MP sensor - it is a much better camera than any phone.<br/>To put resolution in perspective, a 4K video’s resolution is about 8.3MP. Instagram’s largest resolution is a 1.2MP square. Most cameras have a 24MP sensor. In reality, unless you’re cropping heavily, like to pixel-peep, or are printing large images, megapixels don’t really matter. However, just because it doesn’t produce much of a practical effect in the end, doesn’t mean that it’s worthless. If you want higher fidelity images just for yourself, then that’s a perfectly valid reason to want more resolution.</dd>

</div>

</dl>

Do also note that the camera bodies only reflect half of the story. The larger the sensor is, the more expensive the lenses will be.

### A used camera can save money

Many older cameras still work well. Compare used prices on sites such as eBay, MPB, and KEH. Check the condition, shutter count if listed, return policy, included battery and charger, lens mount, and sensor.

</div>

<div aria-label="Used camera checklist" class="grid gap-px bg-white/10 sm:grid-cols-3 lg:grid-cols-1">

<div class="space-y-2 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<strong class="block text-neutral-100!">Check the listing</strong>

Confirm the model, included accessories, visible wear, and any listed damage.

</div>

<div class="space-y-2 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<strong class="block text-neutral-100!">Check the system</strong>

Check lens prices before buying the body. A cheap body is not a bargain if its lenses cost too much.

</div>

<div class="space-y-2 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<strong class="block text-neutral-100!">Check the return policy</strong>

Buy from a seller with a good rating and a clear return policy.
