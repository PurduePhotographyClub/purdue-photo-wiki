Whether you’re just getting into photography or upgrading from an older camera, trying to make a good decision is very hard - camera gear is expensive, and you don’t want to regret your purchase in a few months’ time. This section will aim to provide a few suggestions for cameras in different price brackets, as well as help you look for and decide what features are important to you. Remember, just because a camera isn’t mentioned here doesn’t mean it isn’t good. Try to learn what matters to you, and if a camera checks all those boxes for a reasonable price, then go for it!

## What should I look for?

The importance category should only be a rough guide, because everyone’s priorities are different. What matters to you varies greatly depending on your standards, what type of photography you do, and your budget, so don’t take these labels as gospel.

<table>

<thead><tr><th scope="col">Name</th><th scope="col">Importance</th><th scope="col">Notes</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c38020875dc99fcb77c8cc"></span>Cost</td><td>Above all</td><td>Cost decides everything. Some cameras are strictly better than others, but it all depends on what fits within your budget.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-3027b3bf66c380008c86c5da60a08eb2"></span>New vs. Used</td><td>Important</td><td>Many cameras age very well and are also much cheaper when used. The general recommendation is to almost always buy used through sites like eBay, MPB, or KEH.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c3809288bdc69712065c52"></span>In-body image stabilization</td><td>Important</td><td>IBIS allows you to achieve much slower handheld shutter speeds, increasing your low-light effectiveness greatly.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c380c48cccd64847040e85"></span>Sensor Size</td><td>Important</td><td>Sensor size is an extremely hotly debated topic that involves a lot of misinformation and requires an understanding of technical details. Generally, smaller sensor cameras will be cheaper and have smaller lenses, in return for lesser image quality. Read more in <a href="/photography/technical/">Technical</a>.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c380aaabbbf8c5d6ce6257"></span>Size/Weight</td><td>Important</td><td>The importance of size/weight depends on each person’s individual preferences. For many people, the lighter the camera, the more likely they are to take it out and use it - which is, of course, one of the best things a camera can do.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c3809ab54dc65012bcd719"></span>Autofocus</td><td>Important</td><td>Autofocus importance varies greatly. Many people can get away with the most basic autofocus that puts a box on a still subject, and it will suit their needs. Other photographers, like sports photographers, depend wholly on good autofocus.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c380039994e0da1bf609f2"></span>Mount</td><td>Important</td><td>Your camera’s mount is extremely important (some say it is more important than the camera itself), and dictates the selection of lenses you get to choose from. Most brands have their own mount where only their own lenses (and perhaps some third-party lenses) will work. You will have to do your own research here.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-3027b3bf66c38098944ed05a96bf1942"></span>DSLR/Mirrorless</td><td>Important</td><td>DSLR stands for Digital Single Lens Reflex, which means that the viewfinder is optical - it is an actual projection of reality using mirrors. Mirrorless cameras have electronic viewfinders (EVFs) and have largely replaced DSLRs, meaning that they are more modern but also more expensive.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c380548db7c6d211a019e0"></span>Dynamic Range</td><td>Nice to have</td><td>Dynamic range is particularly important to landscape photographers, and may not be as noticeable to others. Play with your raw files to pull the shadows up and the highlights down.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c380a8b158fb44e1bb45b2"></span>Megapixels</td><td>Nice to have</td><td>1MP = 1,000,000 pixels. A very common misconception that many people draw from reading spec sheets and advertisements is that more megapixels = better images. However, this is not the case: the iPhone 17 Pro has a 48MP main sensor, while the Nikon Z8 has a 45MP sensor - it is a much better camera than any phone.<br />To put resolution in perspective, a 4K video’s resolution is about 8.3MP. Instagram’s largest resolution is a 1.2MP square. Most cameras have a 24MP sensor. In reality, unless you’re cropping heavily, like to pixel-peep, or are printing large images, megapixels don’t really matter. However, just because it doesn’t produce much of a practical effect in the end, doesn’t mean that it’s worthless. If you want higher fidelity images just for yourself, then that’s a perfectly valid reason to want more resolution.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c3805f96e6f434e97ad905"></span>Ergonomics</td><td>Nice to have</td><td>Good ergonomics make photography more enjoyable and make you want to go out and take pictures more.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c38060afe3e91e6d1604bb"></span>Viewfinder</td><td>Nice to have</td><td>Many people never even use the viewfinder, and only rely on the screen. This is all up to you, but a nice viewfinder is good for fast action, immersion, and bright daylight.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c380b5b77cd6761fdf9e09"></span>Weather Sealing</td><td>Nice to have</td><td>Most cameras are not IP rated, but many are built to withstand varying amounts of rain or dust. Whether this matters to you depends on what/where/when you shoot. Do remember that you also need to pair a body with a weather-sealed lens.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c3802bb028d4fe04a4c3e5"></span>Battery Life</td><td>Nice to have</td><td>More battery life is always appreciated, but extra batteries are not hard to find and carry in your bag.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c380f7a6dad85100d17506"></span>Screen type</td><td>Nice to have</td><td>Most cameras include either a fully articulating screen or a tilting screen. Fully-articulating screens are more flexible, especially for video, but may be a little tedious to use. Tilting screens are quick and convenient, but aren’t as versatile.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c380dd96b3e3e99fb7f1cc"></span>SD Card Slots</td><td>Specialized</td><td>Professional cameras have multiple SD card slots for redundancy, in case one fails. This will not be a problem for most people who aren’t professionals, as your paycheck does not depend on your SD card failing.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c380e08631fd20c61b84c2"></span>Shooting Rates</td><td>Specialized</td><td>How fast the camera can capture images (measured in FPS) is not a problem for most people except sports and nature photographers.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c380f19e48db75dead0df2"></span>Rolling Shutter</td><td>Specialized</td><td>Rolling shutter refers to how cameras have to read out information from the sensor line-by-line, and creates that weird wobbling effect seen in videos of helicopter blades. Modern cameras read out their sensors fast enough for 99% of <i>photography</i> use cases, and if this is something that significantly affects your general photography, you probably already know it.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c380b59e14d535ef6b8d5c"></span>Buffer</td><td>Specialized</td><td>Sports and nature photographers need to capture a lot of images at high frame rates, which the camera needs to store before writing to the card.</td></tr>
<tr><td><span id="entry-2807b3bf66c380f6a743fc903ccb197d"></span>Pixel-shift modes</td><td>Specialized</td><td>Some cameras have a special mode that takes several images with shifts in the sensor by a minuscule amount and then combines them to create a higher resolution image. This only works for static scenes; otherwise, you will get ghosting artifacts.</td></tr>
</tbody>
</table>

## Recommendations

This section will contain a list of common recommendations for several budgets. Remember, there are <b>many</b> very good cameras out there, but this list will just contain a few of them. Feel free to ask for clarification or help in Discord!

These prices are MSRP, but you can expect new prices to drop with sales, and used prices to be significantly less depending on the age of the camera. For cameras no longer sold new, prices are rough estimates based on <b><u>used MPB prices</u></b> as of February 2026.

Do also note that the camera bodies only reflect half of the story. The larger the sensor is, the more expensive the lenses will be.
