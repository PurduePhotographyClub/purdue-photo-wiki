## Parts of a Camera

<div class="grid min-w-0 items-start gap-8 lg:grid-cols-2 lg:gap-10">

<div class="space-y-5 min-w-0">

Every camera has a different control scheme, and I can’t cover every single brand and every single camera from each brand. Instead, I will try to cover more generalized information that will be applicable to most cameras, but it’s up to you to look up your camera’s manual or watch videos/read articles that go over your specific camera.

For this article, I will stick to using the Sony A7IV for reference, a relatively new and popular professional-grade full-frame camera.

### Dials

The A7IV has 4 dials in total, all on the right side. This is a little unusual - most cameras will only have 3, but this shouldn’t make much of a difference.

<div class="photography-control-row grid gap-4 border border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5 lg:grid-cols-[6rem_minmax(0,1fr)]">

<div class="min-w-0 space-y-6">

<b>(1)</b>

</div>

<div class="min-w-0 space-y-6">

This is your mode dial. This adjusts what mode your camera is shooting in, choosing from PASM (as mentioned in <a href="/photography/basics/">Basics</a>), Auto, and usually a few custom modes that you can adjust in camera.

</div>

</div>

<div class="photography-control-row grid gap-4 border border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5 lg:grid-cols-[6rem_minmax(0,1fr)]">

<div class="min-w-0 space-y-6">

<b>(2) (3) (4)</b>

</div>

<div class="min-w-0 space-y-6">

Next, (2) is your front dial and typically adjusts exposure compensation, aperture, or shutter speed. (3) is your back dial, and will adjust one of the three settings that (2) does not cover. (4), as mentioned before, is not typically present on most cameras, but it can be customized to adjust whatever you want.

</div>

</div>

Going back to what we covered earlier in the previous article, my recommendation for most people who aren’t doing wildlife or sports is aperture priority with auto ISO. With this setup, you can use the front dial to adjust your aperture and the back dial to adjust your exposure compensation, which is a simple and convenient control scheme that will take you as far as you want it to. Optionally, on a camera like the A7IV you can use the extra back dial to adjust ISO manually, or set it to something else.

</section>

<b>(9)</b>

<b>(10)</b>

<b>(11)</b>

</div>

<div class="min-w-0 space-y-6">

AF-ON (9) is used for a method of focusing called Back Button Focusing, which means that instead of half-pressing your shutter button to autofocus as many people normally do, some people prefer to separate these two buttons and use the AF-ON button to activate focus instead. AEL (10) stands for Auto Exposure Lock, and it does exactly what it says: it locks the camera’s auto-metering, so that if you move your scene around it won’t readjust. Lastly, the autofocus joystick (11) is used to adjust the camera’s autofocus point. This is usually reserved for more expensive cameras aimed at professionals, and there is a good chance your camera might not have this.

<div class="min-w-0 space-y-6">

This is the camera’s hot shoe, which is used to mount various accessories to the camera, such as a flash or microphone. When it doesn’t have any electronic connection to the camera, it is called a cold shoe.

</div>

</div>

<div class="photography-control-row grid gap-4 border border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5 lg:grid-cols-[6rem_minmax(0,1fr)]">

<div class="min-w-0 space-y-6">

<b>(7)</b>

</div>

<div class="min-w-0 space-y-6">

This is the camera’s viewfinder, which is one of two ways you can view and compose your images while shooting. It is essentially a small screen that you can put right up to your eye (and makes you feel like a true photographer!). While being a holdover from film and DSLR days, it still has some advantages over using the screen like a phone. It can help immerse you in the scene by blocking out everything else except your frame, run at higher refresh rates or brightness levels compared to the screen, or help you see your frame easier in bright daylight, among other reasons. Some people simply prefer the experience. Many cameras have the viewfinder in the middle of the camera like a DSLR, but some are modeled after rangefinders, which means that the top is flat and the viewfinder is located off to the left side.

</div>

### In-Body Image Stabilization (IBIS)

<div class="grid min-w-0 items-start gap-8 lg:grid-cols-2 lg:gap-10">

<div class="space-y-5 min-w-0">

IBIS refers to the technology that shifts the sensor in your camera to compensate for any hand movements that will shake the camera - basically, it’s an internal gimbal. This helps with video, but also with low-light photography. IBIS is one of the most crucial elements in letting you achieve sharp shots at night without a tripod. Without IBIS, the shot below would have been impossible to achieve, and with multiple tries the slowest shutter speed I could have used here would have been around 1/40s or so. Compared to the 1/5s I was able to get, that’s 3 stops - people pay a <i>lot</i> more money for much smaller differences in terms of camera image quality and lens maximum aperture.

You almost always want to have IBIS enabled, unless you’re shooting in broad daylight where your shutter speeds will undoubtedly be fast enough and you want to maximize your camera’s battery life.

</div>

### Raw vs. JPG

<div class="grid min-w-0 items-start gap-8 lg:grid-cols-2 lg:gap-10">

<div class="space-y-5 min-w-0">

One of the best ways to squeeze more out of your camera is to shoot in RAW, which you can enable in the camera’s settings (you can choose to shoot JPEG, RAW, or RAW + JPEG). While JPEGs are flat, lossy files that contain only the information you see when you open them in an image viewer, RAW files contain the raw sensor output from the camera. What this means is that when you open it up in Lightroom or other editing software, you have much greater control and can pull up otherwise lost shadows or blown out highlights. The trade-off is that RAW files won’t look as good unedited (they bypass the camera’s internal JPEG color processing), and they can be much larger in size. Shoot RAW if you like to edit, or JPEG if you prefer to leave them more untouched.

It is also perfectly fine to edit JPEGs, as you can easily get by without RAWs unless you need to do some heavy post-processing in high dynamic range scenes.

</div>

Body parts

## Main parts of the camera body

These parts decide which lenses and accessories fit, how the camera records light, and where it saves files.

<div aria-label="Physical camera parts" class="anatomy-component-grid grid grid-cols-1 border-l border-t border-neutral-800 lg:grid-cols-2">

<div class="space-y-2 border-b border-r border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<span class="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-300">Lens mount</span>
<strong class="block font-['Playfair_Display',Georgia,serif] text-lg text-neutral-100">Where the lens locks on</strong>

The mount holds the lens at the right distance from the sensor. Its electronic contacts control autofocus, aperture, and stabilization.

</div>

<div class="space-y-2 border-b border-r border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<span class="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-300">Sensor</span>
<strong class="block font-['Playfair_Display',Georgia,serif] text-lg text-neutral-100">Where light becomes data</strong>

The sensor records the image. Larger sensors often give more room in low light and more control over depth of field. The lens and your technique still matter.

</div>

<div class="space-y-2 border-b border-r border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<span class="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-rose-300">Shutter</span>
<strong class="block font-['Playfair_Display',Georgia,serif] text-lg text-neutral-100">How long light is recorded</strong>

A mechanical or electronic shutter controls exposure time. This is the part behind the shutter speed explained in Basics.

</div>

<div class="space-y-2 border-b border-r border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<span class="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-emerald-300">Hot shoe</span>
<strong class="block font-['Playfair_Display',Georgia,serif] text-lg text-neutral-100">Accessory connection</strong>

Use it for a flash, trigger, microphone, or other accessory. Some brands also send digital audio or control data through it.

</div>

<div class="space-y-2 border-b border-r border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<span class="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-300">Card slot</span>
<strong class="block font-['Playfair_Display',Georgia,serif] text-lg text-neutral-100">Where files are saved</strong>

Fast cards help with photo bursts and video. After backing up the files, format the card in the camera.

</div>

<div class="space-y-2 border-b border-r border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<span class="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-300">Battery</span>
<strong class="block font-['Playfair_Display',Georgia,serif] text-lg text-neutral-100">Powers the camera</strong>

Mirrorless cameras use power for the screen, viewfinder, autofocus, and stabilization while you shoot.

</div>

</div>

Finder and screen

## Viewfinder or rear screen?

The <span class="anatomy-term anatomy-term--cyan font-bold text-sky-300">viewfinder</span> blocks everything outside the frame. It is often easier to see in bright daylight and can have a brighter, smoother display than the rear screen.

Many cameras put the viewfinder in the middle. Rangefinder-style cameras usually have a flat top and put it on the left.

The <span class="anatomy-term anatomy-term--amber font-bold text-amber-300">rear screen</span> works well for low and high angles, video, menus, and touch focus. A fully articulating screen flips out and rotates, which helps when filming yourself.

<div aria-label="Viewfinder and screen comparison" class="anatomy-summary-grid grid grid-cols-1 border-l border-t border-neutral-800 lg:grid-cols-2">

<div class="space-y-2 border-b border-r border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<span class="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-sky-300">Viewfinder</span>
<strong class="block font-['Playfair_Display',Georgia,serif] text-lg text-neutral-100">Frame at eye level</strong>

Easy to see in bright light and steady against your face.

</div>

<div class="space-y-2 border-b border-r border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<span class="text-xs font-bold uppercase tracking-wider text-amber-300">Rear screen</span>
<strong class="block font-['Playfair_Display',Georgia,serif] text-lg text-neutral-100">Frame from more angles</strong>

Useful for touch controls, video, tripods, and low or high viewpoints.

## Settings to find first

You do not need to learn every menu. Start with these settings.

<div aria-label="Important beginner camera settings" class="anatomy-settings-list grid grid-cols-1 border-l border-t border-neutral-800 lg:grid-cols-2">

<div class="space-y-2 border-b border-r border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<strong class="block font-['Playfair_Display',Georgia,serif] text-lg text-neutral-100">IBIS / stabilization</strong>

Leave it on while shooting by hand. It reduces camera shake at slow shutter speeds, but it cannot freeze a moving subject.

</div>

<div class="space-y-2 border-b border-r border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<strong class="block font-['Playfair_Display',Georgia,serif] text-lg text-neutral-100">Autofocus mode</strong>

Use single AF for still subjects. Use continuous AF for people, sports, wildlife, and anything moving toward or away from you.

</div>

<div class="space-y-2 border-b border-r border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<strong class="block font-['Playfair_Display',Georgia,serif] text-lg text-neutral-100">Subject detection</strong>

If your camera detects eyes, faces, animals, vehicles, or birds, add the setting to a quick button or menu.

</div>

<div class="space-y-2 border-b border-r border-neutral-800 bg-neutral-950 p-5">

<strong class="block font-['Playfair_Display',Georgia,serif] text-lg text-neutral-100">Raw / JPEG</strong>

Choose Raw for more editing room. Choose JPEG for smaller files that look finished straight from the camera.

</div>
