## Use what you have

Use your phone or camera. Learn how it handles bright light, shadows, motion, and color before buying a lens.

</div>

## Try different light

Shoot near a window, at sunset, under neon, on a cloudy day, and at noon. Compare the results.

Move closer or farther away. Check the corners. Remove anything that pulls attention from the subject.

In fact, one of the best cameras available to you is one you already own: your phone. Although its diminutive size and distinctive “phone look” can prove to be discouraging factors, phone camera technology has advanced extremely far and can still produce great images. Don’t feel that you <i>need</i> an actual camera to join the club. Many of our current members just use their phones, and having a good eye for composition, light, and color will help you go far.

In the case that you do want to upgrade to a real camera, there are many budget options that won’t break the bank, giving you great features and image quality for a few hundred dollars. You can find an in-depth buying guide over at <a href="/photography/buying-guide-gear/">Buying Guide/Gear</a>, but don’t go there just yet - it will be very overwhelming at first, and unhelpful if you don’t know what you want.

<aside class="border border-purple-200/30 bg-purple-200/[0.05] p-6 sm:p-8 space-y-5">

<strong>As opposed to traditional art, photography is more about <i>capturing</i> an image, rather than <i>creating</i> one from scratch. This is what makes it special.</strong>

Photography trains your eye to see the beauty present within both our daily lives and our special moments, and your camera helps capture these within your own expression. As you keep doing photography, you’ll eventually develop your own style where you notice that you tend toward certain subjects, colors, or environments. A well-trained photographic eye will always be alert, constantly searching for interesting compositions. Even without a camera in your hand, just searching for photos in your mind every day will help you become a better photographer.

</aside>

## Find what you like to photograph

Cameras get expensive fast. First, notice what you enjoy photographing: portraits, cars, concerts, campus at night, or small details. That will tell you what gear you need.

When you are ready to buy, read the <a href="/photography/buying-guide-gear/">gear guide</a>. Until then, keep practicing.
