  <section class="gs-hero">
    <div class="gs-hero__copy">
      <p class="gs-kicker">No gatekeeping, no gear panic.</p>
      <p class="gs-lead">
        So you want to get into photography, huh? Perfect. Start with the camera you already have, take a walk, and give yourself permission to make a few weird photos.
      </p>
      <p>
        You do not need a "real" camera to join the club. Plenty of good photos begin on a phone. What matters first is noticing things: the color of late afternoon light, a reflection in a window, the way someone stands in a doorway, the little frame hiding inside a normal day.
      </p>
    </div>

  <section class="gs-practice" id="gs-practice" aria-label="What to practice first">
    <div class="gs-practice__copy">
      <p class="gs-practice__lead">
        Your first few goals are simple: notice <em class="accent-amber">light</em>, pick a <em class="accent-sky">frame</em>, and make the photo feel <em class="accent-purple">intentional</em>.
      </p>
      <div class="gs-practice__step">
        <span>01</span>
        <div>
          <h2>Use What You Have</h2>
          <p>Your phone is enough. Learn how it sees highlights, shadows, motion, and color before you worry about lenses.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="gs-practice__step">
        <span>02</span>
        <div>
          <h2>Chase Light</h2>
          <p>Good light does a lot of the heavy lifting. Try windows, sunsets, neon, cloudy days, and harsh noon light just to see what happens.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
      <div class="gs-practice__step">
        <span>03</span>
        <div>
          <h2>Make It Intentional</h2>
          <p>Move your feet. Crop with your body. Ask what belongs in the frame and what can leave.</p>
        </div>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="gs-practice__image" aria-hidden="true"></div>
  </section>

  <section class="gs-section gs-section--split" id="gs-gear">
    <div>
      <p class="gs-kicker">Before buying gear</p>
      <h2>Figure Out What You Like Shooting</h2>
      <p>
        Cameras get expensive fast, and specs can turn into soup if you do not know what you want yet. Spend a little time noticing your taste first. Portraits? Cars? Concerts? Campus at night? Random sidewalk details? That answer tells you more than a YouTube gear tier list.
      </p>
      <p>
        When you are ready, the <a href="/photography/buying-guide-gear/">Buying Guide / Gear</a> page can help. Until then, the better upgrade is usually practice.
      </p>

    <p class="gs-kicker">Club member work</p>
    <h2>Phone, Camera, Whatever. Train Your Eye.</h2>
    <p>
      Photography is different from drawing a blank canvas into existence. Most of the time, the world is already doing something interesting. Your job is to notice it, choose a frame, and press the button at the right moment.
    </p>
